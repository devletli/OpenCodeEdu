## 5️⃣ Recommended next step

1. **Validate willingness to pay** before any code or product creation.  
   *Action:* Post a **minimum‑viable‑price test** on skillbay.sh’s “sell” section (or a simple Gumroad/Indiehunt link) at $15, $20, and $25. Track how many “buy” clicks or email sign‑ups you receive over a 2‑week window.  
   *Why:* If < 5 % of the 25 bounty respondents express intent, the venture’s demand hypothesis is falsified.

2. **Differentiate on niche, not price** – e.g., bundle the skill pack with a **Google‑Slides API script** and a **before‑after case study** of a real B2B sales deck. This adds tangible value beyond “template‑only” packs that already exist on Beautiful.ai/Slidebean.

3. **Consider a subscription model** (e.g., $5 /mo for updates + new templates) instead of a one‑off fee, aligning with user expectations from existing SaaS tools.

4. **If the test shows > 10 % intent**, proceed to build the SKILL.md pack, upload it to skillbay.sh, and add a link from the Claude Marketplace (if a consumer‑pricing path opens).  

5. **Document the outcome** in the venture journal; use the data to decide whether to keep, pivot, or kill the venture (freeing a slot for a higher‑conviction opportunity).

### Bottom line
- **Demand is weak/unproven** – a single $100 bounty is the only concrete signal.  
- **Competition is strong** and priced on a monthly subscription basis, making a $15‑$25 one‑off skill pack an uphill sell.  
- **Historical precedent** shows similar agent‑skill packs have been killed because the free‑skill audience does not convert to paying customers.  

**Recommendation:** Run the minimal‑price test (step 1). If the test fails to generate meaningful interest, **kill the venture** and free the slot for a higher‑conviction opportunity. If it shows unexpected demand, reconsider with a subscription‑style offering and stronger differentiation.
